# Fatigue and Drowsiness Guide

## Understanding Fatigue vs Drowsiness
- **Fatigue:** A general feeling of tiredness and reduced energy. You may feel mentally or physically depleted but can still maintain alertness if needed.
- **Drowsiness:** A state of near-sleep where maintaining wakefulness is actively difficult. Characterized by heavy eyelids, frequent eye closures, and head drooping. Drowsiness is more dangerous than fatigue because it can lead to involuntary microsleep episodes.

## Immediate Actions for Drowsiness

### Critical Safety Warning
If you are drowsy while driving, operating machinery, or performing any safety-critical task, STOP IMMEDIATELY. Drowsy performance is comparable to impaired performance. Pull over, take a break, or hand off the task. No deadline is worth risking your safety.

### Quick Alertness Boost
1. **Stand up immediately** — the change in posture sends alertness signals to your brain
2. **Splash cold water on your face** — activates the mammalian dive reflex which increases alertness
3. **Step outside or open a window** — fresh air and natural light help reset your circadian signals
4. **Do 20 jumping jacks or walk briskly** — physical activity increases blood flow and alertness

## Short-Term Fatigue Management

### The 20-20-20 Rule (Screen Fatigue)
Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet (6 meters) away for 20 seconds. This reduces eye strain which is a major contributor to perceived fatigue during screen work.

### Micro-Nap (Power Nap)
If you have the opportunity:
- Set an alarm for 10-20 minutes (not longer)
- Find a quiet, comfortable spot
- A 10-20 minute nap improves alertness, cognitive function, and mood for 2-3 hours
- Avoid napping longer than 30 minutes — this leads to sleep inertia (grogginess)
- Do not nap after 3 PM as it may interfere with nighttime sleep

### Caffeine Strategy
- Caffeine takes approximately 20-30 minutes to take effect
- Effective dose: 100-200 mg (roughly 1-2 cups of coffee)
- Avoid caffeine within 6 hours of your planned bedtime
- The "nappuccino" technique: drink a coffee immediately before a 20-minute nap — the caffeine kicks in as you wake up, providing maximum alertness

### Stretching Routine (2 minutes)
1. Neck rolls: 5 circles left, 5 circles right
2. Shoulder shrugs: raise shoulders to ears, hold 3 seconds, drop — repeat 5 times
3. Seated spinal twist: twist torso left (hold 10s), then right (hold 10s)
4. Wrist circles: 10 in each direction
5. Stand and touch toes (or reach toward them)

## Preventing Future Fatigue

### Sleep Hygiene Fundamentals
- **Consistent schedule:** Go to bed and wake up at the same time daily (even weekends)
- **Sleep duration:** Most adults need 7-9 hours per night
- **Screen cutoff:** Avoid screens 30-60 minutes before bed (blue light suppresses melatonin)
- **Room environment:** Cool (65-68°F / 18-20°C), dark, and quiet
- **Avoid alcohol before bed:** It fragments sleep architecture even though it may help you fall asleep faster

### Daytime Habits
- Expose yourself to bright natural light within 30 minutes of waking
- Stay hydrated — dehydration causes fatigue
- Avoid heavy meals that cause post-meal drowsiness
- Take regular movement breaks throughout the day
