# Blood Pressure Management

## Overview
Blood pressure measures the force of blood pushing against your artery walls. Maintaining a healthy blood pressure is crucial for cardiovascular health and overall well-being. Consistently high blood pressure (hypertension) can lead to serious health complications if left unmanaged.

## Healthy Ranges
- **Normal:** Systolic < 120 mmHg and Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- **Elevated:** Systolic 120-129 mmHg and Diastolic < 80 mmHg
- **High Blood Pressure (Hypertension Stage 1):** Systolic 130-139 mmHg or Diastolic 80-89 mmHg

*Note: Short-term spikes in blood pressure are normal during exercise, stress, or excitement. Chronic high blood pressure requires management.*

## Immediate Actions for Acute Spikes
If your estimated blood pressure is slightly elevated due to stress or acute physiological strain:
- **Stop and Rest:** Sit down in a comfortable position and take a break from what you are doing.
- **Deep Breathing:** Practice slow, deep abdominal breathing (e.g., box breathing or 4-7-8 breathing) for 3-5 minutes to activate the parasympathetic nervous system.
- **Hydrate:** Drink a glass of water. Dehydration can sometimes affect blood pressure.
- **Grounding Exercise:** Focus on your surroundings (5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, etc.) to reduce anxiety.

## Long-Term Management Strategies
- **Dietary Changes:** Adopt the DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet, focusing on fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins while reducing sodium intake.
- **Regular Exercise:** Engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week (e.g., brisk walking, cycling, or swimming).
- **Stress Reduction:** Incorporate daily stress management techniques such as meditation, yoga, or progressive muscle relaxation.
- **Limit Stimulants:** Reduce caffeine and alcohol consumption, as they can elevate blood pressure.
- **Adequate Sleep:** Ensure 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night, as poor sleep is linked to hypertension.

## When to Seek Help
Consult a healthcare professional if:
- Your blood pressure is consistently above 130/80 mmHg over several days or weeks.
- You experience severe headaches, shortness of breath, chest pain, or visual changes (seek immediate medical attention).
- You have a family history of hypertension or cardiovascular disease.
