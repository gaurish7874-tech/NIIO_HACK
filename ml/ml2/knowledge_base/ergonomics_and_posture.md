# Ergonomics and Posture

## Overview
Proper ergonomics and posture are essential for preventing musculoskeletal pain and reducing physical fatigue during long periods of sitting or screen use. Poor posture can manifest as unstable gaze, frequent head adjustments, and head drooping.

## Key Indicators of Poor Ergonomics
- **Head Drooping (High Pitch):** Looking down at a screen or phone puts significant strain on the cervical spine.
- **Restless Head Movements (High Yaw):** Frequently adjusting your head position may indicate neck discomfort or an improperly placed monitor.
- **Slouching:** Compresses the lungs and diaphragm, leading to shallow breathing and decreased oxygen flow, which can increase feelings of fatigue.

## Immediate Adjustments
- **Check Monitor Height:** The top of your screen should be at or slightly below eye level. Use a stand or books to raise it if necessary.
- **Fix Your Chair:** Sit with your back straight, shoulders relaxed, and lower back supported. Use a cushion if your chair lacks lumbar support.
- **Foot Position:** Your feet should rest flat on the floor or on a footrest. Avoid crossing your legs for extended periods.
- **Elbow Angle:** Keep your elbows close to your body and bent at an angle between 90 and 120 degrees while typing.

## Movement and Breaks
- **Micro-breaks:** Stand up, stretch, or walk around for 1-2 minutes every 30 minutes.
- **Neck Stretches:** Gently tilt your ear toward your shoulder on each side and hold for 15 seconds to relieve neck tension.
- **Posture Reset:** Periodically squeeze your shoulder blades together and hold for 5 seconds to counteract the forward-hunching posture.

## When to Seek Help
Consult a physical therapist or doctor if:
- You experience persistent neck, back, or wrist pain that doesn't improve with ergonomic adjustments.
- You have numbness, tingling, or weakness in your hands or arms.
