# Sleep Hygiene

## Overview
Sleep hygiene refers to healthy habits and environmental factors that promote consistent, uninterrupted, and restorative sleep. Poor sleep hygiene is a primary cause of daytime fatigue, drowsiness, and elevated stress markers.

## Impact on Wellness Signals
- **Fatigue and Drowsiness:** Poor sleep directly correlates with increased eye closure duration, slow blink rates, and head drooping.
- **Stress:** Chronic sleep deprivation elevates resting heart rate and blood pressure, mimicking a physiological stress response.

## Core Sleep Hygiene Principles
- **Consistent Schedule:** Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, even on weekends. This reinforces your body's circadian rhythm.
- **Optimize Your Environment:** Ensure your bedroom is cool (around 65°F/18°C), quiet, and completely dark. Consider blackout curtains, earplugs, or a white noise machine.
- **Digital Curfew:** Avoid screens (phones, tablets, TVs, computers) for at least 1-2 hours before bed. The blue light emitted by screens suppresses melatonin production.
- **Limit Stimulants and Alcohol:** Avoid caffeine in the late afternoon and evening. While alcohol might make you feel sleepy initially, it disrupts the later stages of restorative sleep.

## Pre-Sleep Routine
- **Wind Down:** Establish a relaxing pre-sleep routine, such as reading a physical book, taking a warm bath, or practicing gentle stretching.
- **Brain Dump:** If racing thoughts keep you awake, write down your tasks or worries on a piece of paper to get them out of your mind.
- **Avoid Heavy Meals:** Try not to eat large, heavy meals or spicy foods right before bedtime to prevent indigestion and discomfort.

## When to Seek Help
Consult a doctor or sleep specialist if:
- You consistently have trouble falling or staying asleep despite good sleep hygiene.
- You feel excessively tired during the day even after sleeping 7-9 hours.
- You or a partner notice loud snoring, gasping, or pauses in your breathing during sleep (signs of sleep apnea).
