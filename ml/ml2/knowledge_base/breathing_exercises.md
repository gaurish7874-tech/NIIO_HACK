# Breathing Exercises Guide

## Why Breathing Matters
Your breathing pattern directly influences your heart rate, heart rate variability, and overall stress level. Slow, controlled breathing activates the vagus nerve and shifts your nervous system from sympathetic (fight-or-flight) to parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) mode. This is one of the few direct voluntary pathways to control your autonomic nervous system.

## Quick Reference: Which Technique to Use

| Situation | Best Technique | Time Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Acute anxiety / panic | Physiological Sigh | 30 seconds |
| General stress | Box Breathing | 2-4 minutes |
| Before sleep / deep calm | 4-7-8 Breathing | 3-5 minutes |
| Ongoing tension | Diaphragmatic Breathing | 5-10 minutes |
| Hyperventilation | Extended Exhale | 2-3 minutes |

## Technique 1: Physiological Sigh (Fastest Anxiety Relief)
Discovered by Stanford neuroscientist Dr. Andrew Huberman. The fastest voluntary method to reduce autonomic arousal.

**Steps:**
1. Inhale through your nose
2. At the top of the inhale, sneak in a second quick inhale (a "top-off" breath)
3. Exhale slowly and fully through your mouth (make the exhale longer than the inhale)
4. Repeat 1-3 times

**Why it works:** The double inhale maximally reinflates collapsed alveoli in your lungs. The long exhale slows the heart rate through the vagus nerve. Even one cycle produces measurable stress reduction.

## Technique 2: Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)
Used by military special forces and first responders for rapid calm under pressure.

**Steps:**
1. Inhale through your nose for 4 counts
2. Hold breath for 4 counts
3. Exhale through your mouth for 4 counts
4. Hold (lungs empty) for 4 counts
5. Repeat for 4-6 cycles

**Tip:** If 4 seconds feels too long, start with 3-3-3-3 and work up. The rhythm is more important than the duration.

## Technique 3: 4-7-8 Breathing
Developed by Dr. Andrew Weil. Particularly effective for calming before sleep.

**Steps:**
1. Exhale completely through your mouth with a whooshing sound
2. Close your mouth and inhale quietly through your nose for 4 counts
3. Hold your breath for 7 counts
4. Exhale completely through your mouth for 8 counts
5. Repeat for 4 cycles

**Note:** The ratio (4:7:8) matters more than the absolute duration. If 4-7-8 seconds is too long, try 2-3.5-4 seconds instead.

## Technique 4: Diaphragmatic (Belly) Breathing
The foundation of all breathing exercises. Ensures you are using your diaphragm rather than shallow chest breathing.

**How to check if you are doing it right:**
1. Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly
2. When you inhale, your belly hand should rise MORE than your chest hand
3. If your chest rises first, you are chest breathing (common during stress)

**Steps:**
1. Sit or lie comfortably
2. Place one hand on your belly
3. Inhale slowly through your nose for 4 seconds — feel your belly push your hand out
4. Exhale slowly through pursed lips for 6 seconds — feel your belly fall
5. Continue for 5-10 minutes

## Technique 5: Extended Exhale (For Hyperventilation)
When breathing too fast (>20 breaths per minute), the priority is to slow the exhale.

**Steps:**
1. Inhale normally through your nose (don't force a deep breath)
2. Exhale for TWICE as long as you inhaled (e.g., inhale 3 seconds, exhale 6 seconds)
3. Pause briefly at the bottom of the exhale (1-2 seconds)
4. Repeat until breathing rate normalizes (target: 12-16 breaths per minute)

**Why it works:** Extended exhales activate the parasympathetic nervous system through the vagus nerve, slowing heart rate and reducing the sense of breathlessness.
